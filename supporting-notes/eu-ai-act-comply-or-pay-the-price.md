# Supporting Notes – The EU AI Act: Comply or Pay the Price

## Why the EU AI Act matters to cybersecurity
- The regulation introduces mandatory obligations, not optional guidance.
- AI systems must be inventoried, classified, and governed like other high-risk technologies.
- Security teams will be responsible for evidence, monitoring, and incident handling linked to AI use.

## Practical steps organisations should take now
1. Build an AI inventory covering tools, vendors, use cases, data sources, and integrations.
2. Classify AI systems by risk, business impact, and regulatory exposure.
3. Assign clear ownership for AI risk, compliance, and security controls.
4. Map EU AI Act obligations to existing ISMS, GDPR, and risk management frameworks.
5. Prepare audit-ready evidence such as logs, documentation, and control testing.

## Security and governance controls to consider
- Data classification and restrictions for AI inputs and outputs
- Third-party AI vendor due diligence and contract controls
- Monitoring for data leakage, misuse, and abnormal AI behaviour
- Access control and segregation for AI tools and integrations
- Incident response playbooks updated to include AI-related events

These notes support the published article and provide a practical starting point for organisations preparing for EU AI Act compliance.
